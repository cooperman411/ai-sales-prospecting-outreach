# Outbound email writing and review

A human message, not a synthetic sales pitch:

- Research one relevant, **verifiable** reason to reach out.
- Write a short, descriptive subject, not a fake reply or urgency tactic.
- Begin with the real business context. Acknowledge uncertainty rather than
  asserting that the company has a pain point you cannot substantiate.
- Explain one useful capability in ordinary words. Avoid fake testimonials,
  inflated claims, flattery, sales clichés and over-personalized trivia.
- Ask one easy, relevant question. A clear no-pressure decline is acceptable.
- Typical first note: about 70–140 words **before required compliance footer**;
  this is an editorial starting point, not a measured deliverability rule.
- Use real sender identification, required postal address, and an effective,
  easy opt-out mechanism for commercial email. If these are not available,
  prepare a draft but do **not** send.

**Optional low-volume sequence** (requires approval of timing, content, and
recipient eligibility): first email, one relevant follow-up around day 5–7,
and an optional final note around day 12–16. These are editable starting points,
not optimized or mandatory cadences. Stop immediately on reply, opt-out, bounce
or meeting. Never schedule automated follow-ups from a draft creation date.

**Review package:** recipient and evidence of role/email; chosen send-from
identity; full subject/body/signature; personalization source URLs; legal and
suppression status; selected delivery times; maximum sends; edit/version
fingerprint; and all changes to be made in CRM or sequencing tools.

No auto-send. Creating drafts is distinct from sending. A model may prepare
multiple drafts in one approved batch, but each send must match reviewed copy.
